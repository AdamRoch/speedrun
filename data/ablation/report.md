# The probe ablation: does remembering the card mean knowing the thing?

**Every learner in this experiment is simulated. No human data appears anywhere in this
document.** Probes were generated by the shipped pipeline's deterministic no-AI `--baseline`
generator (`pylib/anki/probe_gen.py`), not by a model; results against AI-generated probes would
need a separate, labelled run (`just ablation-ai`, requires `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, not
reproducible bit-for-bit).

All numbers below come from one run of the harness (`just ablation`, seed 42) and are stored
verbatim in [`results.json`](results.json). Re-running the same command reproduces the same
numbers bit for bit; the unit test `tiny_run_is_deterministic_and_off_equals_stock` in
`rslib/src/ablation.rs` guards this property at small scale.

## 1. The claim, and what would falsify it

Ascent's central claim: _a study tool that measures only whether you remember the card cannot
tell you whether you know the thing._ The probe — a reworded variant served when the scheduler
is already confident — is the instrument that is supposed to expose the difference.

Pre-stated falsification conditions (also hard-coded in `rslib/src/ablation.rs::FALSIFICATION`
and evaluated mechanically by the harness, before any results were looked at):

1. **Gap condition.** On reviews the scheduler is confident about (FSRS retrievability ≥ 0.85),
   if the pass rate on reworded probes is within 5 points of the pass rate on the original
   cards — or the 95% CI of the difference includes zero — the probe adds no information and
   the claim is unsupported.
2. **Brier condition.** If FSRS retrievability predicts held-back _probe_ outcomes at least as
   well as it predicts held-back _card_ outcomes (Brier(R→probe) ≤ Brier(R→card)), then
   remembering the card already tells you whether you know the thing, and the claim is false.
3. **Validity gate.** If the feature-off arm does not schedule identically to the stock arm,
   the experiment is invalid regardless of outcome. During development a second validity gate
   was added in the same spirit (stricter, never looser): if the leakage check of §5 fails,
   `run_valid` is false regardless of outcome.

## 2. Method

### The three arms

| Arm       | What it is                           | How it is realised                                                                                         |
| --------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **stock** | Upstream Anki behaviour              | Same binary, probes deleted from the collection; the serving path short-circuits on the empty probes table |
| **off**   | Ascent, feature present but disabled | Probes attached, `probe_rate = 0`                                                                          |
| **on**    | Ascent, probes served                | Probes attached, `probe_rate = 0.5`, retrievability threshold 0.85                                         |

All three arms run the **real** `Collection`: the real FSRS scheduler (stock default
parameters, never fitted), the real queue builder, and the real probe-substitution branch
(`Collection::maybe_probe_substitute`). Nothing in the scheduler, FSRS, or the serving branch
was modified for this experiment. The deck configuration is identical across arms except for
`probe_rate`.

### Simulated learners

Each of the 8 learners per arm studies the same 200 synthetic fact cards for 150 simulated
days (time is advanced by shifting the collection's creation stamp and review timestamps, so
FSRS sees ordinary day arithmetic). Ground truth per (learner, card):

- **Surface memory** — what the original card's cue retrieves — follows an FSRS-shaped
  forgetting curve with a private stability trajectory (initial stability U[0.5,4.0]d, growth
  ×U[1.6,3.2] per success, collapse ×0.3 on failure, per-learner ability factor U[0.8,1.25]).
- **Transfer** — how much of that retention survives a reworded cue. 40% of (learner, card)
  pairs are _rote_ (transfer U[0.05,0.35]); the rest are _understood_ (transfer U[0.65,0.95]).
- P(pass original card) = R_true. P(pass probe) = transfer × R_true. First exposure is a study
  event, always graded Good. Pass → Good, fail → Again. FSRS short-term relearning steps are
  answered but excluded from recall metrics (they are re-study events minutes after a failure).

This is a _model of a world in which rote knowledge exists_. The experiment tests whether the
instrument can detect it — see §6 for what this does and does not establish.

### Fair-testing setup

- **Held-back split.** Every 5th card (40 of 200) is held back. Held-back outcomes are used
  only for the calibration metrics below; no harness parameter, threshold, or design choice was
  tuned on them. FSRS runs with stock default parameters, so no model was fitted to anything.
- **Probe provenance.** Probes are generated from the cards by
  `just probe-gen out/ablation/corpus.anki2 --deck Default --baseline` — the same pipeline and
  quality gate (`gate_reject_reason`) that production uses, with the deterministic no-AI
  generator.
- **Leakage check** (adapted from `data/mcat-taxonomy/report.md` §5.3): see §5.

## 3. Results

All numbers from `results.json` (seed 42, 8 simulated learners/arm, 200 cards, 150 days;
re-running produced a bit-identical file).

### Validity gate: feature-off ≡ stock

The off and stock arms produced **identical review sequences** for all 8 learners — same
cards, same days, same outcomes, same post-answer interval, due date, and FSRS stability bits
(`identical_review_sequences: true`, 10,894 recall reviews + 504 relearning steps each,
pass rate 0.8379 in both). The on-arm difference below is therefore attributable to the probe
alone. (One control was applied uniformly to _all three_ arms: the FSRS load balancer is
disabled, because it selects intervals from the histogram of earlier answers, which makes
scheduling sensitive to within-day serve order that the harness cannot hold fixed.)

### The gap: scheduler-confident cards still fail when reworded

Within the on arm, among reviews where the scheduler's own retrievability estimate was ≥ 0.85,
the serving coin randomizes whether the original card or the probe is shown — a randomized
within-arm comparison:

| Shown            | n      | pass rate | Wilson 95%     |
| ---------------- | ------ | --------- | -------------- |
| Original card    | 13,943 | **0.906** | [0.901, 0.911] |
| Probe (reworded) | 13,400 | **0.347** | [0.339, 0.355] |

**Gap = 0.559** (95% CI [0.549, 0.568]). The pre-stated falsification threshold was 0.05: not
met, by an order of magnitude. In this simulated world, a card the scheduler is ~91%-confident
about fails almost two times out of three when the same fact is asked in unfamiliar words.

### Rote blindness: card-only data cannot see what probes see

Pass rates split by the ground-truth label of each (learner, card) pair:

| View                  | understood pairs | rote pairs      |
| --------------------- | ---------------- | --------------- |
| Off arm, card reviews | 0.833 (n=6,472)  | 0.844 (n=4,422) |
| On arm, probe reviews | 0.688 (n=4,464)  | 0.177 (n=8,936) |

In the feature-off arm — i.e., in any card-only tool — rote pairs are _indistinguishable_ from
understood ones (0.844 vs 0.833; rote pairs pass slightly **more** often, since surface recall
is what card reviews measure). Probe outcomes separate the two groups by half a probability
unit. This is the claim's mechanism, measured.

### Cost of the treatment

The on arm did 28,646 recall reviews and 7,818 relearning steps against 10,894 and 504 in the
off arm — roughly 2.6× the review load — because probe failures feed back into scheduling as
lapses. The instrument is not free; see §6.

## 4. Calibration against held-back outcomes

Held-back set: every 5th card (40 of 200), excluded from all harness tuning; FSRS parameters
are stock defaults, never fitted to anything. Forecast = FSRS retrievability at serve time.

| Outcome set (held-back cards only) | n     | Brier     | log-loss  | base rate | base-rate Brier |
| ---------------------------------- | ----- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------------- |
| Card outcomes, off arm             | 2,221 | 0.138     | 0.457     | 0.838     | 0.136           |
| Original-card outcomes, on arm     | 2,870 | 0.085     | 0.316     | 0.908     | 0.084           |
| **Probe outcomes, on arm**         | 2,531 | **0.497** | **1.400** | 0.366     | 0.232           |

Reading: FSRS's own retrievability is a serviceable forecast of whether you will pass the
_card_ (Brier 0.138, in line with the base-rate benchmark 0.136). As a forecast of whether you
will pass the _probe_ it is catastrophic — Brier 0.497 is worse than always guessing the base
rate (0.232) and about what coin-calling scores (0.25 would be a coin; 0.497 approaches the
0.5+ region of anti-correlated forecasts): the scheduler says ~0.88 and the truth is ~0.37.
The pre-stated Brier falsification condition (Brier(probe) ≤ Brier(card)) is not met.

This is the quantitative form of the claim: the number the scheduler already computes tells
you about the card, and _nothing in card-only data warns you_ that it says nothing about the
fact. Note the on-arm's _original-card_ Brier (0.085) is actually better than the off arm's —
probe lapses purge overdue cards, so the surviving high-R pool is cleaner. The feature does
not degrade ordinary scheduling calibration.

## 5. Leakage check

Adapted from `data/mcat-taxonomy/report.md` §5.3, run against the probe texts actually stored
by the pipeline. Held-back items are the card and probe texts of the 40 held-back cards;
12,800 (probe, held-back item) pairs were checked with normalized exact matching plus Jaccard
similarity over 3-word shingles.

| Check                                                  | Result                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exact duplicates of a held-back item (risk A)          | **0**                                                                                       |
| Near-duplicates (J ≥ 0.8) vs held-back card questions  | **0** (max J = 0.0)                                                                         |
| Near-duplicates (J ≥ 0.8) vs held-back probe questions | **0** (max J = 0.625, shared template scaffolding)                                          |
| Probe identical to its own source card (risk B)        | none — min distance 1.0 (the baseline generator inverts Q/A, so surfaces share no shingles) |
| Probes containing their own answer (risk C)            | **0** (the pipeline's `gate_reject_reason` also enforces this at generation time)           |

`leakage_check.passed = true`, and this gate is wired into `run_valid` — a contaminated
held-back set invalidates the run outright.

**The check caught a real leak during development.** An earlier corpus draw let two cards
share the same pseudoword answer; the baseline generator inverts question and answer, so it
emitted bit-identical probe questions for both cards — one of them held back
(`exact_duplicates: 1`, run invalidated). The fix was to make the corpus honor its own
"distinct facts" specification (unique subjects and answers), not to weaken the check. This is
also a true finding about the baseline generator: on any deck where two cards share an answer,
Q/A-inversion produces duplicate probes, and §5.3-style screening is not optional.

## 6. What this experiment does and does not establish

**It establishes:**

- The three arms run end-to-end through the real scheduler and the real serving branch, from
  one seeded command, reproducibly.
- Feature-off is behaviourally identical to stock at run scale, so any on-arm difference is
  attributable to the probe alone.
- _Conditional on a world that contains rote knowledge_, card-only review data is blind to it,
  while probe outcomes expose it — with the measured effect sizes above.

**It does not establish:**

- **That human learners have rote knowledge in this proportion, or at all.** The 40% rote
  fraction and the transfer distributions are assumptions of the simulation, not measurements
  of people. The gap's _magnitude_ is a property of those assumptions; only its _detectability
  by the instrument_ is the finding. A human study would be required to measure the real-world
  gap, and nothing here substitutes for it.
- **That probe text quality matters.** Simulated outcomes are driven by a per-card transfer
  parameter, not by reading the probe. A probe that is subtly broken (ambiguous, or leaking its
  answer) would score identically here. The text-level checks in §5 and the pipeline's quality
  gate are the only content-sensitive parts of this experiment, and they are weaker evidence
  than human answering behaviour would be.
- **That the on-arm's extra reviews are worth their cost.** Probe failures feed back into
  scheduling (the card is treated as lapsed), so the on arm does more work. Whether
  the exposed rote cards justify the added review load is a product question this experiment
  does not answer.
- **AI probe quality.** The committed run uses the deterministic baseline generator. AI-written
  probes ride the identical path but were not exercised here (no API key in the run
  environment, and their generation is not reproducible).

## 7. Reproducing

```
just ablation            # baseline probes, deterministic, writes data/ablation/results.json
just ablation-ai         # AI probes (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY required; not bit-reproducible)
```

Both accept `--seed N --learners N --cards N --days N`. The harness lives in
`rslib/src/ablation.rs`; the corpus/probe collection is built in `out/ablation/`.
